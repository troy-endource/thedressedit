Body shape guides have been around forever, and most of them are terrible. They reduce women to fruit metaphors, imply that certain body types need to be "corrected," and offer advice so generic it's essentially useless. We're not doing that here.

Instead, this guide is built on a simpler premise: different dress shapes have different effects on the body, and understanding those effects lets you make choices that work for you — whether that means emphasising your waist, elongating your frame, creating balance, or simply feeling comfortable. There's no "wrong" body shape, and there's no "right" way to dress. There's just information, and what you do with it is entirely up to you.

## Before We Start: A Note on Body Positivity

Every body shape guide risks implying that some bodies need "fixing." Let's be clear: they don't. The purpose of understanding dress shapes isn't to hide or disguise anything — it's to make getting dressed easier and more enjoyable. If you love how you look in a particular dress, wear it regardless of what any guide says. These are suggestions, not rules.

That said, most of us have had the experience of trying on a dress that looked incredible on the hanger but somehow didn't translate to our body. That's not your body's fault — it's a mismatch between the dress's cut and your proportions. Understanding why helps you avoid those disappointments and find the styles that make you feel brilliant.

## The Universal Fits

Before getting into specific body shapes, three dress styles that work on virtually everyone:

**The wrap dress** is the closest thing to a universally flattering silhouette. The crossover bodice creates a V-neckline (elongating), the tie waist defines the smallest part of your middle, and the A-line skirt skims rather than clings from the waist down. It works on tall, petite, slim, and curvy bodies because the structure naturally adjusts to your proportions.

**The A-line midi** — fitted through the bodice and gradually widening from the waist or hips — flatters by creating a balanced silhouette regardless of your proportions. It's the dress shape equivalent of a reliable friend: always appropriate, never disappointing.

**The shirt dress** with a defined waist (either belted or tailored) works across body types because it combines structure with ease. The collar frames the face, the defined waist creates shape, and the relaxed skirt provides comfort. It's the only dress that works equally well for work, weekends, and weddings.

## Hourglass Shape

**What it is:** Bust and hips are roughly similar in measurement, with a noticeably narrower waist in between. This is the proportional shape that most fitted dresses are designed for, which means many cuts will feel natural.

**What works brilliantly:** Anything that defines the waist. Wrap dresses, belted midis, pencil dresses, fit-and-flare silhouettes. Your proportions are naturally balanced, so dresses that acknowledge and follow your shape will always look polished. V-necklines and scoop necks flatter beautifully by echoing the natural lines of your frame.

**What to be careful with:** Very boxy or oversized shapes can make you look shapeless rather than relaxed, because they obscure the waist that creates your silhouette. If you want a relaxed fit, belt it — even loosely — to retain some definition. Stiff fabrics that don't drape with your curves can also feel awkward; look for fabrics with some movement.

**Best high street brands for this shape:** Reiss, Karen Millen, Diane von Furstenberg (especially the wrap dress), and Mango's satin collection.

## Pear Shape

**What it is:** Hips wider than shoulders, with weight carried predominantly below the waist. One of the most common body shapes in the UK and one that the fashion industry has historically underserved — but that's changing.

**What works brilliantly:** A-line and fit-and-flare dresses are your friend — they skim over the hips rather than clinging. Dresses with interesting necklines, sleeve details, or upper-body embellishment draw the eye upward and create visual balance. Square necklines, puff sleeves, and statement collars all do this effectively. Dark or solid colours below the waist, with lighter or more detailed fabric above, create an elegant proportional effect.

**What to be careful with:** Bodycon or very fitted skirts that cling to the hips can feel uncomfortable and highlight the area where the proportional difference is greatest. This isn't about hiding your hips — it's about choosing cuts that skim rather than grip, which is more flattering and more comfortable. Tulip skirts and dropped waists can also create an unbalanced line.

**Best high street brands for this shape:** & Other Stories (beautiful fit-and-flare cuts), COS (structured A-lines), Nobody's Child, and LK Bennett.

## Apple Shape

**What it is:** Weight carried predominantly around the middle, with slimmer legs and arms. The key with dressing an apple shape is creating a sense of flow through the torso rather than drawing attention to the midsection.

**What works brilliantly:** Empire-line dresses (fitted under the bust, flowing below) are exceptional — they define the narrowest point above the waist and let the fabric drape freely. V-necklines elongate the torso beautifully. Column dresses in fluid fabric that skim rather than cling create a sleek, unbroken line. Shirt dresses worn slightly open at the neck with a loose belt at the hip (not the natural waist) work wonderfully.

**What to be careful with:** Wide belts at the natural waist can create a visual pinch point that's unflattering. Very structured or stiff fabric that holds away from the body in the middle section can add visual bulk. Peplums are divisive — some apple-shaped women love the definition they create, others feel they draw attention to the midsection. Try before you decide.

**Best high street brands for this shape:** ARKET (relaxed, draped cuts), Mint Velvet (brilliant fluid dresses), H&M's viscose collection, and Whistles.

## Rectangle Shape

**What it is:** Shoulders, waist, and hips are roughly similar in width, creating a straight silhouette without a dramatically defined waist. This is actually one of the most versatile body shapes for fashion, because most modern, minimalist dress designs are cut for a straighter frame.

**What works brilliantly:** You can go two directions. To create waist definition, try wrap dresses, belted styles, or peplum waists — anything that introduces a curve. To lean into the straight silhouette, column dresses, shift dresses, and relaxed shirt dresses all look incredibly chic on a rectangular frame. Layering (a dress with an unbuttoned shirt or a structured jacket) also adds visual interest and dimension.

**What to be careful with:** Very fitted dresses that are designed to follow curves may not have enough to follow, leaving them looking flat. Instead, create shape through structure — a dress with architectural details, pleating, or a defined shoulder creates visual interest that's more effective than relying on body-hugging fabric.

**Best high street brands for this shape:** COS (designed for straighter frames), & Other Stories, ARKET, and Cos's minimalist aesthetic is essentially built for this body shape.

## Inverted Triangle Shape

**What it is:** Shoulders wider than hips, often with a larger bust. The visual emphasis is in the upper body, and the goal (if you want one) is to add visual weight below the waist to create balance.

**What works brilliantly:** Full skirts, A-lines, and anything with volume from the waist down balance broader shoulders beautifully. V-necklines and scoop necks are more flattering than boat necks or high necklines, which can emphasise shoulder width. Dark colours on top with lighter or printed fabric below shift the visual focus downward.

**What to be careful with:** Puff sleeves, wide necklines, and heavily embellished bodices all add visual width to an already broader upper body. Raglan sleeves and halter necks can also emphasise shoulder width. Strapless dresses are divisive — they can look spectacular but require excellent structural support to work well.

**Best high street brands for this shape:** Reiss (excellent V-neck designs), Phase Eight (brilliant for occasion dresses that balance proportions), Hobbs, and Boden.

## Petite

**What it is:** Under 5'3" (160cm), regardless of body shape. The challenge isn't your body — it's that most high street dresses are cut for someone 5'5"–5'7", which means hemlines, proportions, and waist placements often sit in the wrong place.

**What works brilliantly:** Buy petite ranges whenever possible — the proportions are adjusted throughout, not just the length. Higher waistlines, V-necklines, and vertical details (front buttons, centre seams, long necklaces) all elongate visually. Midi dresses that hit at the shin rather than mid-calf work best — too long and they can swamp a petite frame.

**What to be careful with:** Oversized silhouettes can overwhelm a smaller frame. Very wide maxi dresses, especially in busy prints, can make you look shorter. Keep proportions in mind: smaller prints generally work better than large-scale ones.

**Best high street petite ranges:** M&S Petite (the widest range), ASOS Petite, Topshop Petite, and John Lewis's petite edit.

## Finding Your Brands

The most practical advice in this entire guide: once you find a brand that consistently fits your body well, stick with it. Different brands cut for different bodies — that's not a flaw, it's a feature. COS tends to cut for straighter, less curvy frames. Reiss is excellent for defined waists. M&S has the widest range of fits, including petite, tall, and curve collections. & Other Stories suits most shapes but runs slightly small in the bust.

Finding "your" brands saves more time and money than any body shape guide ever will.

## The Bottom Line

The best dress for your body is the one that makes you feel confident when you catch your reflection. Body shape guides are useful shortcuts for navigating a fitting room, but they're not rules. Try things on. Trust the mirror over the theory. And if you love it, wear it — no guide should override how a dress makes you feel.

The question of what to wear, which once felt like the fun part, starts to feel like a logistical challenge. ## The New Rules of Wedding Guest Dressing

Before we get to the dresses, a quick refresher on what's changed — and what hasn't. The old rules about avoiding white, being cautious with black, and dressing for the venue rather than yourself still absolutely apply. But 2026 has introduced a few shifts worth noting.

**The midi has firmly overtaken the maxi** as the go-to length for daytime ceremonies. It's practical, flattering across body types, and works with both heels and flats — essential when half of summer's best weddings seem to be on grass. A hemline that hits mid-calf gives you the elegance of a long dress without the trip hazard on uneven terrain.

**Rich jewel tones have made a comeback** for autumn and winter weddings, with burgundy, emerald, and sapphire replacing the pastels that dominated last year. This is great news if you find pastels wash you out — deeper colours tend to photograph beautifully in low light and feel more intentional.

**Texture is the new embellishment.** Rather than all-over sequins or heavy beading, the most interesting wedding guest dresses this season use fabric texture to create interest: pleated silk, crinkled crepe, jacquard weaves, and subtle sheer panels. These details look beautiful in person and in photos, without trying too hard.

**The jumpsuit is now fully accepted** at all but the most traditional ceremonies. A beautifully tailored wide-leg jumpsuit in a rich fabric can look every bit as polished as a dress, and often more so. If you prefer trousers, this is your moment.

## Summer Wedding Dresses: Light, Bright and Breezy

Summer weddings call for colour, movement and — crucially — breathable fabric. This is where cotton and linen earn their keep, though a well-cut viscose or Tencel blend will keep you cool without creasing on the journey. We're looking for dresses that feel effortless without looking underdressed.

For outdoor ceremonies in May and June, a floral midi in cotton or linen is hard to beat. Keep the print sophisticated — watercolour florals, ditsy prints, or painterly botanicals all work beautifully — and pair with block heels or wedges you can walk on grass in. A crossbody bag keeps your hands free for champagne.

For July and August weddings, when temperatures genuinely climb, prioritise fabric over everything else. A simple slip dress in a cooling satin, or a relaxed shirt dress in lightweight cotton, will keep you comfortable through an afternoon ceremony and into evening dancing. Avoid anything too structured or lined — seams and layers trap heat.

### Colours that work for summer

Soft greens (sage, pistachio, olive), warm pinks (blush, terracotta rose, coral), sky blue and lavender are all strong choices for summer 2026. Butter yellow has emerged as the standout colour of the year — it's warm, joyful, and flatters a wide range of skin tones. If you're attending multiple weddings, a dress in a solid colour is easier to restyle with different accessories than a bold print.

## Formal & Black Tie: The Elegant Edit

When the invitation says black tie, resist the urge to panic-buy something you'll never wear again. The smartest approach is to invest in a dress that can be restyled across multiple events — a well-cut column dress in navy or emerald, for instance, transforms with different jewellery and shoes each time.

Look for fabrics that drape rather than cling: silk, satin, and crepe will all move beautifully and photograph well in evening light. A bias-cut slip dress in a jewel tone is endlessly versatile — add a velvet wrap for winter formality, or let it stand alone in summer with statement earrings.

Keep embellishment considered. A single pair of chandelier earrings or a beaded clutch often does more work than all-over sequins. The goal is to look elegant and polished, not like you're competing with the bride's sparkle.

### The three-event test

Before investing in a formal dress, ask yourself: can I wear this to at least three different events? A navy satin midi passes this test easily — it works for a winter wedding, a work Christmas party, and a milestone birthday dinner. A dress covered in novelty sequins probably doesn't. Cost per wear is everything when you're spending over £200.

## Country & Barn Weddings: Relaxed Sophistication

The country wedding is arguably the trickiest dress code to get right. Too formal and you'll look overdressed against hay bales and bunting; too casual and you'll feel underdone next to the mother of the bride. The sweet spot is a dress with structure but softness — think a cotton midi with interesting sleeves, a printed tea dress, or a linen wrap style in a warm tone.

Florals are your friend here, but aim for ditsy or watercolour prints rather than anything too bold or tropical. Earthy tones — olive, terracotta, mustard, dusty rose — tend to complement rural settings beautifully and look gorgeous in golden-hour photography.

Practicality matters more at country weddings than anywhere else. You may be walking across fields, sitting on straw bales, or navigating a tipi on grass. Choose shoes you can genuinely walk in (wedge espadrilles, block-heel sandals, or even smart flats), and consider whether your dress allows you to sit down, climb into a vintage bus, and dance without restriction.

## City Weddings: Sharp, Modern, Memorable

London hotel, Manchester loft, Edinburgh townhouse — city weddings call for something with a bit more edge. This is where you can experiment with sharper silhouettes, bolder colours, and modern fabrications. The urban backdrop rewards confidence: a block-colour midi in cobalt or fuchsia, an architectural one-shoulder dress, or a sleek tailored jumpsuit will all hold their own.

The beauty of a city wedding is that you can lean into contemporary style without worrying about practicality the way you would in a field. Heels are fine on polished floors. Structured fabrics like heavy crepe and bonded jersey hold their shape in air-conditioned rooms. And a clutch bag is perfectly practical when you're not trekking between venues.

For an evening city wedding, don't be afraid of drama. A bold lip with a simple dress, or statement earrings with a clean neckline, creates the kind of put-together look that suits metropolitan settings. Think about what you'd wear to the best restaurant in town, then elevate it one notch.

## Autumn & Winter: Rich Tones & Luxe Fabrics

Late-season weddings are an invitation to lean into richer, more dramatic dressing. Velvet, heavy crepe, and textured knits replace the linens and cottons of summer, while colours deepen from pastels to jewel tones. Burgundy, forest green, navy, and aubergine all work beautifully, and a well-chosen metallic — bronze, champagne gold — adds warmth without overwhelming.

Layering becomes essential for autumn and winter weddings. A beautifully cut coat or faux-fur stole is as much a part of your outfit as the dress itself — and will feature prominently in any outdoor photographs. Consider this when choosing your palette: does your coat complement your dress? Do your accessories tie the look together?

Sleeve length matters in cooler months. A long-sleeved dress in a rich fabric — velvet, heavy silk, or textured crepe — feels inherently more polished than adding a cardigan over a strappy summer dress. If you prefer bare arms, invest in a statement cover-up that becomes part of the outfit rather than an afterthought.

### Fabrics for the cold

**Velvet** is the hero fabric for winter weddings. It drapes beautifully, looks rich in photographs, and provides just enough warmth to take the edge off a draughty church. A velvet midi in emerald or plum is the kind of dress you'll reach for year after year.

**Heavy crepe** is a smart alternative if velvet feels too seasonal. It has beautiful drape and holds colour intensely, making jewel tones look especially luxurious.

**Satin and silk** work for evening winter weddings, but choose heavier weights (charmeuse rather than habotai) so the dress doesn't cling in the cold or look too summery.

The trick is to look for simple, well-cut shapes in good fabrics. A plain satin midi will always look more expensive than a heavily embellished polyester number. Clean lines, a flattering waist, and quality fabric — these are the three things that separate a £50 dress that looks like £200 from a £50 dress that looks like £50.

## What Not to Wear: The Definitive List

Some things never change. Here's what to avoid:

- **White, ivory, cream, or anything close to bridal.** Unless the couple explicitly asks guests to wear white, steer well clear. This includes "off-white," "champagne," and "pale blush."

- **Anything too revealing.** Super short hemlines, very deep necklines, or completely backless styles risk shifting the focus. Save the bodycon for date night.

- **All-black at a daytime garden wedding.** Black works beautifully at evening and winter weddings, but can feel funereal at a sunny afternoon ceremony. Break it up with colourful accessories.

- **Anything you haven't tried sitting down in.** You will sit through a ceremony, a meal, and probably several speeches. Always do the sitting test before committing.

- **Dresses that exactly match the bridal party.** If you know the bridesmaids are wearing sage green, choose a different shade.

- **Jeans. Ever.** Even if the invitation says casual. "Casual" at a wedding means "relaxed but put together."

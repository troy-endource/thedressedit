The problem with buying a party dress is that "party" covers an enormous amount of ground. A dinner for twelve at someone's house is a party. So is a summer wedding in a marquee, a work celebration in a private room, a birthday at a restaurant you've wanted to go to for years. The dress that's right for one of those occasions can look completely wrong for another — and most people end up buying something new each time, which is both expensive and unnecessary.

The better approach is to understand what kind of occasion you're actually dressing for, and then find a dress that's built for it. endource's edit of over 60 party dresses — spanning Simone Rocha, Self-Portrait, Rixo, Victoria Beckham, Reiss, STAUD, and more — covers the full range. Browse the full selection at [endource](https://www.endource.com/edit/the-best-party-dresses/5sigq71bcjw9).

## The dinner party dress

A dinner means you'll be sitting down for most of the evening, which changes the requirements considerably. The dress needs to look good from the waist up, work in a candlelit room rather than a dance floor, and not require constant adjustment. Fabric that holds its shape matters more here than at any other occasion — jersey that bags, or a skirt that rides up when you sit, defeats the purpose.

**Victoria Beckham** is the clearest answer to this brief. The Stretch-Jersey Zip-Up Midi Dress is built around a fabric that moves with the body and returns to its original shape — it's a dress you can wear for a four-hour dinner and still look as good leaving as arriving. The Gathered Draped Jersey Turtleneck Midi Dress takes the same logic and adds a more sculptural drape at the neckline, which gives it a more deliberate evening quality.

**Toteme's** Slouch Short-Sleeves Woven Midi Dress offers a different approach — less body-conscious, more architectural. The relaxed shoulder and woven fabric give it a quality that photographs well and wears with ease. **Joseph's** Ortiz Pleated Woven Dress similarly prioritises construction over decoration: the pleating is precise, the fabric substantial, and the overall effect is of something that belongs at a smart dinner without announcing itself.

**STAUD** contributes two pieces that work well here. The Freda Ruched Silk-Blend Gown has a gathered bodice that creates a softly draped effect — dressed up enough for a special occasion, relaxed enough not to feel like a performance. The Joy Cotton-Blend Faille Maxi Dress is more structured and works for evenings where the occasion is more formal.

## The wedding and celebration dress

Weddings, milestone birthdays, christenings — occasions where the dress code calls for something more elevated and where you want the dress to do some of the work. These are pieces with more presence: more deliberate fabric choices, more considered construction, more likely to be remembered.

**Simone Rocha** has four pieces in this part of the edit, and each one represents the designer's particular approach to occasion dressing. The Tulle Midi Dress layers tulle in a way that creates volume without weight — it's a dress that moves distinctively and suits occasions where you want to look properly dressed. The Lace-Trimmed Sheer Midi Dress is more restrained, with the lace detail at the hem giving it a finish that sits clearly within occasion wear without tipping into costume. The Embroidered Tulle Midi Dress is the most elaborate of the three, with embroidery across the body that makes it the piece for the most significant occasions.

**Saloni's** Camille Bow-Embellished Velvet Gown is the most traditionally glamorous piece in the edit — a full-length velvet dress with bow detail that suits black-tie events or particularly formal celebrations. **TOVE** offers two different expressions of occasion dressing: the Tova Gathered Crêpe Maxi Dress, where the gathered bodice creates a softer silhouette, and the Naeve Ribbed Chenille Maxi Dress, which is more structured and works through cooler months.

**DÔEN's** Coraline Corded Lace-Trimmed Silk-Satin Maxi Dress sits somewhere between romantic and refined — the lace trim and silk-satin fabric give it a quality that suits weddings, while the relaxed maxi length means it travels well and avoids the stiffness of more formal gowns. **Magda Butrym's** Cotton and Silk Minidress is at the other end of the length spectrum: a mini with the kind of construction that makes it work at an occasion where a shorter dress would typically feel underdressed.

**Khaite's** Daria Satin-Crepe Turtleneck Mini Dress is the most understated investment piece in the edit — the satin-crepe has a weight and finish that sits at the luxury end of the edit, and the turtleneck detail gives it a formality that works at smart evening occasions. **Reiss** contributes seven pieces across the edit, and several sit in this category: the Alicia Petite Dress is a good example of the brand's approach — clean lines, good fabric, designed to work across multiple occasions without looking like it belongs specifically to one of them.

## The evening out dress

Bars, clubs, late dinners, birthday celebrations where the evening extends well past midnight. The brief here is different: you want a dress that works under low lighting, holds up across a long evening, and has enough presence to feel right for the occasion without requiring a complete change of accessories or shoes.

**Self-Portrait** dominates this section with six pieces, and that makes sense — the brand has spent years refining its approach to dressing for exactly this occasion. The Crystal-Embellished Mesh Midi Dress is the most dressed-up of the group: mesh with crystal embellishment that catches light without being gaudy. The Belted Embellished Cotton-Blend Lace Midi Dress is more wearable for a wider range of occasions — the lace and belt give it structure, and the cotton-blend makes it more comfortable over a long evening than pure synthetic alternatives. The Taffeta Minidress and the Appliquéd Crepe Mini Dress both take a shorter approach, with fabric and detail doing the work that length might otherwise do.

**Simkhai** contributes two sequined options: the Isla Sequined Halterneck Minidress and the Hope Bow-Detail Taffeta Minidress. Both are designed specifically for evenings where more decoration is appropriate — the sequins on the Isla work particularly well under artificial lighting.

**COS's** Metallic Wool-Blend Mini Dress is the most surprising piece in this section. Wool-blend might not sound like evening wear, but the metallic finish gives it a quality that works at night — and the warmth of the fabric makes it a practical choice for spring and autumn evenings when a satin mini would leave you cold. **& Other Stories** offers three more accessible options: the Keyhole-Collar Sequin Mini Dress, the Lace Frill Mini Dress, and the V-Neck Velvet Mini Dress — all three are well-made for the price and suit occasions where you want to be dressed for the evening without a significant outlay.

## What to look for

Fabric choice matters more for party dresses than almost any other category, because occasion wear is typically worn under conditions that expose its limitations — artificial lighting, warm rooms, long evenings. Satin and silk catch light well but show every movement and can be unforgiving in warm conditions. Velvet and taffeta have more body and create more presence, but suit cooler months. Jersey and crepe are more forgiving and more comfortable over long periods, which is why the Victoria Beckham and STAUD pieces in this edit are such reliable choices for dinner occasions.

Neckline is the detail that does most of the work in occasion dressing. A turtleneck (Khaite, Victoria Beckham) adds formality and works particularly well under cold lighting. An off-shoulder neckline (Self-Portrait's Polka-Dot Off-Shoulder Cotton Midi Dress) creates the impression of occasion dressing even at a lower price point. A halterneck (Simkhai) works specifically for evening occasions and suits warm venues. If the occasion involves sitting down for most of the evening, necklines that photograph well from the front matter more than anything below the waist.

Length, for party dresses, is primarily a question of occasion rather than preference. Mini lengths suit evenings out and informal celebrations. Midi lengths cover the widest range of occasions. Maxi lengths suit the most formal occasions and outdoor settings where the ground surface is unpredictable.

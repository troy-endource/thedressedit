## What makes an affordable dress look considered

Before the picks, a few principles worth knowing. These are the things that separate a £45 dress that reads as £150 from a £45 dress that reads as £45.

**Fabric first.** Clean fabric in a simple silhouette always outperforms complicated design in cheap material. When shopping at this price point, look for viscose, Tencel, cotton poplin, linen blend, or crepe on the label. Anything with more than 50% polyester will feel and photograph accordingly.

**Simple silhouettes photograph best.** Solid colours and clean lines look intentional in photographs. Over-embellished or heavily ruffled dresses can read as busy in professional wedding photography where every detail is visible. When in doubt, go simpler.

**Colour does considerable work.** A dress in a considered colour — sage, powder blue, dusty rose, terracotta — always looks more deliberate than a generic print. Colour signals that you've thought about what you're wearing, which is the foundation of looking well-dressed.

**Fit matters more than budget.** A perfectly fitting £40 dress will outperform an ill-fitting £300 one every time. The most effective upgrade at any price point is a well-fitting garment.

## Where to shop: a brand guide

Not every brand on endource is equally strong for wedding guest dressing at this price point. Here's where to focus.

**COS (£65–£115):** The most reliable option for understated, well-made occasion dressing. Their pleated and column dresses are some of the best on the market at any price. Architectural cuts, considered fabric weight, excellent colour palette. Size tends to run generous — worth trying before buying if possible.

**& Other Stories (£55–£120):** More expressive than COS — floral wrap dresses, interesting sleeve details, prints that feel artistic rather than generic. Their wrap dresses are consistently among the most flattering on the high street, and fabric quality in their printed viscose pieces is noticeably good.

**Mango (£30–£80):** Quietly one of the best places for affordable occasion dresses. Their satin and crepe pieces regularly produce the moment where someone can't believe what you paid. Clean lines, good drape, a design sensibility that feels slightly more elevated than most UK high street options.

**ARKET (£55–£95):** Understated and genuinely well made. Their heavy jersey midis hold their shape throughout a full day of wearing and barely need ironing — which makes them excellent for travelling to weddings. Limited colour range, but what's there is always considered.

**Ghost (£55–£150):** Satin and chiffon dresses with a relaxed, romantic quality. Their wrap styles and tie-shoulder midis are particularly good for summer weddings, and they frequently appear at reduced prices on endource.

**Hush (£60–£120):** Relaxed but polished. Column maxis and jersey slip dresses with a quiet confidence that suits country and garden weddings particularly well.

**Whistles (£79–£150):** At the top of this budget, Whistles produces some of the best-value pieces for formal occasions. Their satin and crepe dresses compete directly with brands at double the price.

**Nobody's Child (£35–£75):** Sustainably made, print-forward, and consistently flattering. Their tiered florals are an excellent option for summer and garden weddings.

## The three price tiers

### Under £50

At this price, simplicity is your best asset. Rather than a dress trying to do everything — bold print, elaborate sleeves, heavy embellishment — look for a clean wrap or slip shape in a considered colour. A £45 dress with statement earrings and quality shoes will outperform a £45 dress relying on its own design to do all the work.

### £50 to £75

This is where the edit gets considerably stronger. At this price point you start to find genuinely good fabric — real viscose crepe, cotton with structure, lighter-weight satins that drape properly. The construction holds up to a full day of wearing, and the design details feel deliberate rather than fussy. Most of our favourite everyday wedding guest dresses fall here.

### £75 to £100

At the top of the budget range, you're looking at dresses that compete directly with mid-range brands at £150–200. This is where COS, Whistles, and & Other Stories really deliver — the fabric quality, the cut, and the finishing all step up noticeably. For a more formal wedding or a dress you'll wear across the whole season, stretching to this tier is worth it.

## The cost-per-wear question

Here's the calculation that makes budget dressing genuinely smart. A £300 dress worn once costs £300 per wear. A £60 dress worn to three weddings and two birthday dinners costs £12 per wear. The aim isn't the cheapest dress — it's an affordable dress versatile enough to restyle across multiple events.

The most rewear-friendly dresses share a few things: solid colours rather than memorable prints, classic silhouettes that don't date, and tones that work with accessories you already own. A plain navy or sage midi from COS is almost infinitely restyable. A bold graphic print tends to be more memorable — which is either a feature or a problem depending on how many of the same people you'll be seeing across the wedding season.

## When to buy

Timing your purchase can save another 20–40% on already affordable dresses.

**Late March – early April:** Spring sales clear winter stock and discount early new-season pieces. Good timing for buying wedding season dresses before demand peaks.

**Late June – July:** Mid-season sales offer 20–30% off current summer stock. Useful for August and September weddings.

**Late November:** Black Friday sales are the best time to buy for next year if you already have dates in the diary. COS, & Other Stories, and Mango pieces from the current season often appear at 40–50% off.

## The accessories that complete it

A considered dress with good accessories will always look more intentional than an expensive dress worn without thought. Three pieces worth investing in:

**Statement earrings (£8–25):** The most cost-effective upgrade to any outfit. A sculptural gold drop or an oversized hoop from Mango or & Other Stories transforms a simple dress. This is where your remaining budget is best spent.

**A structured clutch (£15–40):** A clutch in a complementary tone pulls the look together. A slight contrast works better than an exact match — a gold clutch with a sage dress, a burgundy clutch with navy.

**Comfortable shoes you can walk in:** Nobody looks considered limping. Block-heel sandals, kitten heels, or a well-chosen flat all work. The shoes that make you feel most comfortable are the right ones.

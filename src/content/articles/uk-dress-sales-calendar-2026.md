The difference between a considered shopper and everyone else isn't a larger budget — it's timing. The same dress that costs £180 in September is often £90 in January. Multiply that across a year's worth of purchases and you're saving meaningfully without changing what you buy, who you buy from, or how you dress.

This calendar covers the UK dress sale landscape month by month: which retailers discount when, what to expect, and what's worth buying at each point in the year. We update it as sale dates are confirmed, so it's worth checking back before any significant purchase.

## Golden Rules of Sale Shopping

Before the calendar, three principles that will serve you better than any discount code.

**Buy what you'd buy at full price.** The most common sale mistake is purchasing something tolerable because it's reduced. A dress you rarely wear costs more in real terms than a full-price dress you reach for constantly. Before buying, ask the question honestly: would I have wanted this anyway? If not, the percentage off is beside the point.

**Know your sizes across brands.** Sales move quickly and popular sizes disappear within hours of a promotion launching. If you already know you're a 10 in COS, a 12 in & Other Stories, and a medium in Whistles, you can act without hesitating. Keep a note in your phone — it's a small thing that saves a lot of frustration.

**Check the returns policy.** Most UK retailers offer full refunds on sale items, but some offer only exchanges or credit notes. Check before buying, particularly for higher-value pieces.

## The Email Newsletter Strategy

The most reliably useful piece of advice in this guide: sign up for newsletters from the brands you shop most. Most offer 10–15% off your first order as an immediate benefit. Beyond that, newsletter subscribers typically receive early access to sales — sometimes 24–48 hours before the general public — plus flash sale alerts that aren't widely advertised, and occasional exclusive discount codes during quieter retail periods.

The brands where this is most valuable: COS (their sale is genuinely strong), & Other Stories, Reiss (regular 20% off events for members), and Whistles. Most require nothing more than an email address, and you can unsubscribe once the discount is used.

## The Month-by-Month Calendar

### January: Post-Christmas Clearance

The largest sale period of the year. Retailers are clearing autumn/winter stock at significant reductions to make room for spring arrivals, and most brands participate.

**Typical discounts:** 40–70% off autumn/winter collections. Final clearance can reach 80% in the latter weeks.

**Worth buying:** Winter work dresses, darker-toned midis, heavier fabrics, and classic pieces in versatile colours — a navy COS dress or a Reiss shift that works year-round. This is the best month of the year for investment pieces at reduced prices. Avoid summer dresses — stock is thin and you'll have better options later.

**Best brands to check:** COS, Reiss, & Other Stories, Mango, Whistles, AllSaints.

### February: The Quiet Period

The retail lull between sales. Christmas discounts are winding down, spring stock is arriving at full price, and there's relatively little organised discounting. That said, it's a useful time for patient shoppers: end-of-sale clearance can reach rock-bottom prices on remaining winter pieces, and new-season stock is available before it sells out in popular styles.

**Typical discounts:** Final clearance on winter pieces (60–80%). New spring stock at full price.

**Worth buying:** The final winter clearance if you spot something genuinely useful. First spring arrivals if you want new-season pieces before sizing depletes.

### March: Spring Sales Begin

Spring collections are landing and retailers run targeted promotions to drive engagement — 20% off new arrivals, pre-Easter events, and brand-specific sales. It's not as dramatic as January, but it's the most strategic month for buying occasion dresses.

**Typical discounts:** 15–25% off new spring stock via codes and promotions. Remaining winter pieces at 70%+.

**Worth buying:** Wedding guest dresses. March is the optimal window — the widest selection of new-season pieces, enough promotional activity to take some of the price off, and you're buying before the summer wedding rush depletes stock in popular sizes. Spring work dresses are also worth buying now while the range is complete.

**Best brands to check:** Reiss (often runs a friends-and-family event this month), COS, & Other Stories, Whistles.

### April: Easter Promotions

The Easter bank holiday weekend is a consistent UK retail moment. Most brands run site-wide promotions, free delivery offers, and targeted discounts across the long weekend.

**Typical discounts:** 20–30% across most retailers. Some run smaller discounts on full-price items alongside deeper sale reductions.

**Worth buying:** Anything needed for the summer season. Easter is typically the last moment of reasonable pricing before wedding season pushes occasion dress prices to their peak.

**Best brands to check:** Reiss, Whistles, COS, & Other Stories, John Lewis own-brand, Hobbs, Karen Millen.

### May: Wedding Season Begins

Retailers know the demand is there and they're not in a hurry to discount. May through mid-July is the most expensive time of year to buy occasion dresses. Stock is plentiful, but prices are at their peak.

**Typical discounts:** Minimal. Small promotional events but nothing significant.

**What this means in practice:** If you didn't buy during March–April, you'll be paying full price. Focus on pieces you genuinely want and will return to repeatedly rather than anything bought in a hurry.

### June–July: Mid-Season Sales

The first meaningful summer discounting. Retailers run mid-season sales to maintain momentum, clearing early-summer stock and making room for late-season arrivals. COS, & Other Stories, and Mango typically start their summer sales in late June.

**Typical discounts:** 20–40% off spring/summer collections.

**Worth buying:** Summer dresses for August and September occasions. Lighter-weight work dresses. Holiday pieces and anything you want for the tail end of the season.

**Best brands to check:** COS, Mango, & Other Stories, AllSaints, Whistles, Reiss.

### August: Summer Clearance

Deep summer sales as retailers move stock ahead of autumn. If you're flexible on colour and style, August is one of the best-value months of the year for quality pieces at significantly reduced prices.

**Typical discounts:** 40–60% off remaining summer stock. Some items reach 70%.

**Worth buying:** Summer dresses to carry into next year if you spot a reliable style. Transitional pieces in lighter fabrics — viscose midis, cotton shirt dresses — that bridge the gap into autumn. Lighter work dresses.

**Best brands to check:** Nearly all mid-range brands are in sale. COS, Reiss, Whistles, Mango, & Other Stories, AllSaints, Mint Velvet.

### September–October: Transitional Season

New autumn collections arrive at full price while summer clearance continues in the background. A useful period for considered shopping — you can buy end-of-season summer pieces at their lowest prices alongside new arrivals.

**Typical discounts:** Final summer clearance (60%+) and full-price autumn. Some brands run modest new-season promotions.

**Worth buying:** Versatile midi dresses that carry across seasons — heavier jersey, knit dresses, and darker tones that work from October through to spring.

### November: Black Friday

The second major discount event of the year, and increasingly the most useful for planned dress purchases. Most retailers now run promotions for the full week, and some stretch across the entire month.

**Typical discounts:** 20–40% off full-price items. Some brands go deeper. Premium brands that rarely discount — Reiss, Whistles, COS — often participate, which makes this the most useful sale period for quality investment pieces.

**Worth buying:** Investment dresses. The Reiss work midi, the COS column dress, the Whistles wrap you've been considering — Black Friday is when these become accessible. It's also an excellent time to buy ahead for next year's wedding season: a few versatile occasion dresses at 30% off in November saves a great deal of stress in June.

**Best brands to check:** Reiss (consistently strong Black Friday sale), COS, & Other Stories, AllSaints, Whistles, Karen Millen, Phase Eight.

### December: Christmas Promotions & Boxing Day

Pre-Christmas promotions run through the first three weeks of December, followed by Boxing Day sales that carry through into January clearance.

**Typical discounts:** 15–20% in early December promotions, rising to 30–60% in Boxing Day and post-Christmas sales.

**Worth buying:** Occasion dresses for the party season, and any investment pieces missed during Black Friday. The Boxing Day sale bleeds directly into the January clearance, so December and January can be treated as a single extended discount window.

## The Bottom Line

The most useful habit in considered dress buying: purchase one season ahead. Occasion dresses for summer in March. Transitional pieces in August. Investment dresses in November. Planning consistently saves 20–40% and means you're buying from full stock rather than whatever's left in your size at the last minute.
